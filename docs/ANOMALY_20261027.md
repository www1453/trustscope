# Anomaly detection vs the shipped amplitude x area rule, leave-one-board-out (2026-10-27)

Offline only. No board was touched, no bitstream, no `dow`, no `mwr`, no mailbox
write. `ps_ui/main.c` was not modified; nothing under `ps_ui/`, `ps_fft_dual/`, the
STM32 sources or `dl/host_qc/calibrate.py` was run or edited. New files are confined
to `dl/analysis/` plus this report, `dl/anomaly_table_20261027.csv` and `.json`.
Committed with an explicit path list; `git add -A` was not used.

## 0. The answer, with its evidence level

**Conclusion: loses, do not port.**

An anomaly detector trained only on good boards -- either a PaDiM-style per-cell
Mahalanobis score or a PatchCore-style kNN distance, on the cheapest hand-crafted
descriptor that can carry the information -- is **worse than the shipped rule on every
axis that was measured**, at a **lower** cost for one of the two:

| | shipped rule | PaDiM-style | PatchCore-style |
|---|---|---|---|
| false-NG under leave-one-board-out | **1 of 4** | 3 of 4 | 2 of 4 |
| the contaminated board | **caught** (group 9, limit 8) | caught (group 40) | **missed** (group 6) |
| min blob, 50% detection | **24 px** | 32 px | 32 px |
| min blob, 90% detection | 40 px | **not reached** | 40 px |
| min scratch width (96 px long) | **2 px at 0.88-0.92** | never detected | never detected |
| contrast for 24 px blob, 50% | **45 grey** | 90 grey | 90 grey |
| cost | 5.23 M ops/frame | **0.42 M (0.08x)** | 13.5 M (2.58x) |

The one thing the anomaly models cannot do is the thing they were proposed to do:
**detect smaller defects**. The shipped rule's floor is 24 px, and its own geometric
limit (a defect must cover at least 8 connected 8x8 cells) is 22.6 px, so the rule is
already operating at its arithmetic limit. Both models need 32 px, i.e. **about 1.3x
larger area than the rule**, and neither can see a thin scratch at any tested width.

Evidence level **E2** for the numbers in this document: they are offline computations
over frames dumped from the board, and the machine that computes the shipped rule has
been validated against the firmware's own published words (section 2). Evidence level
**E0/E1** for anything about how the models would behave on the board itself -- this
work never ran on the board, and section 11 lists what that leaves open.

### 1.1 `.tmp/synth`, `.tmp/synth2`, `.tmp/synthsum` are not data -- discard

`.tmp/make_synth.py` and `.tmp/make_synth2.py` **generate** those directories: they load
`.tmp/stI/u1/live.raw` and paint a rectangle into it. `.tmp/synthsum/contam/cam0.raw` is
byte-identical to `.tmp/synth2/defect/cam0.raw`, `.tmp/synthsum/shadL/cam0.raw` and
`.tmp/synthsum/shadR/cam0.raw` (all `f9c3ccb2c4bf`), and that image is
`place(500, 420, [(300, 120, -70)])` -- a **40x40 px rectangle painted with -70 grey**
onto unit #1's frame (`make_synth2.py:84`). Its mailbox `MB_DIFF_DAREA = 15` is a
**hardcoded literal** (`part_words(500, 420, darea=15)`, `make_synth2.py:86`), not a
measurement. Likewise `.tmp/synthsum/{goodA,goodA2,goodZ,shad0}` are one frame.
So that directory is a **self-test of the tooling**, and it holds exactly three
distinct synthetic states, not the empty/good/contaminated/shadow series its
sub-directory names promise. Every one of them is excluded from this experiment.

**This is the kind of thing that would have silently produced a fake result**: the
directory is named `contam`, and a script that trusted the name would have "detected
the contaminated board" using an image the experimenter painted.

### 1.2 Locating the contaminated board's frame

It is `.tmp/stG/accept1`, and it is identified by measurement rather than by name:
`dl/STAGE_H_20261025_registration_accuracy.md:33` calls it "the good reference /
defective live pair", and its mailbox publishes `MB_DIFF_DAREA 9`, `MB_DIFF_VERDICT 1`,
`MB_DIFF_DN 60`, `MB_DIFF_FMAXD 113`. That `DAREA 9` matches the value
`dl/STAGE_I_20261025_tolerance_map.md:10-12` records for the defective board
("9..11") under the same fixed threshold.

Note what is **not** on disk: there is **no frame of the contaminated board captured
with the four-unit tolerance map in force**. The only evidence for that state is the
mailbox rows in `dl/STAGE_I_20261025_rows/defect.csv` (28/28 NG, DAREA 14..17). This
experiment therefore measures the contaminated board against the **fixed 30-grey
rule**, not against the tolerance-mapped production rule.

## 2. The measuring instrument, and its known-answer check

The shipped rule is not re-implemented here. `ps_ui/stI_replay.py` is the repo's
cell-for-cell replay of the fine pass (`cell_mean8`, `ref_cell8`, `ref_part_ok`,
`fd = ((live*gain)>>8) - ref - bias`), and this work calls it.

**Known-answer check.** `dl/analysis/population.py` replays every capture that has the
inputs and compares, cell count for cell count, against the numbers the *firmware*
published in the same dump's mailbox:

* **11 of 17 captures reproduce the firmware** within tolerance. The contaminated
  pair is exact: firmware `fvalid 313, dn 60, darea 9, fmaxd 113`; replay
  `309, 58, 9, 113`, on **both** of its frames.
* The 6 disagreements are all captures whose own mailbox says the comparison was
  refused (`MB_DIFF_OK` 2 or 5, `DAREA` 0xFFFFFFFF) or whose masks do not match the
  reference (`stH/unit2` judges 0 cells). They are excluded, not explained away.

Two corrections to the brief's description of the shipped rule, from the source:

* the defect threshold is **`DEFECT_AMP` = 30 grey**, not 14. `FINE_THRESH` = 14 is a
  *different* statistic (`MB_DIFF_FOVER`, the count of cells over 14) and is not the
  defect test -- `ps_ui/stI_replay.py:49-50`.
* the fine cell is **4 samples, each a 3x3 box mean** (`FINE_SAMP` 2, `FINE_SAMP2` 6,
  `FINE_TAP` 3). The 5x5 box is the **16x16 coarse** cell (`SAMP = {16: (4, 12, 2)}`,
  `ps_ui/st8_fine_grid.py:46`). The rest of the brief's summary is right: 8x8 cells,
  green channel, limit `DAREA` 8, `da >= lim` fails (`main.c:10694`).

## 3. The finding that changes the experiment: **the four good units are not four boards**

`dl/STAGE_I_20261025_tolerance_map.md:67` states the tolerance map was learned from
FOUR distinct good units, and `MB_TOL_N = 4` in the mailbox of `u4`. That is the
denominator the whole generalisation claim rests on, so it was tested.

Cell-mean absolute difference after the best integer-cell shift, over the shared part
mask (a **same-board repeat** capture is available for calibration):

| pair | cell-mean abs diff (grey) |
|---|---|
| u4 vs its own repeat capture (`live2`) | **1.31** |
| u3 vs u4 ("two different units") | **1.58** |
| u3 vs its own repeat capture | 1.61 |
| u1 vs map_full | 5.98 |
| u1 vs u2 / u3 / u4 | 11.8 / 12.1 / 11.5 |
| u2 vs the others | 11.4 - 19.9 |

**u3 and u4 differ by less than u3 differs from its own second capture.** They are the
same physical board (or, at minimum, this data cannot distinguish them, and the
difference sits below the same-board noise floor). The naive leave-one-board-out over
u1..u4 would therefore **train on u3 and test on u4** -- the leakage this project has
already been burned by, dressed as a four-fold test.

There is a second, independent hint in the mailbox: `MB_TOL_JUMP` reads 11 for u3 and
12 for u4, and `dl/STAGE_I_20261025_tolerance_map.md:122-126` records that "a repeated
sample of one unit contributed 12 newly-relaxed cells and a genuinely new board
contributed 11..12" -- i.e. u4's contribution looks like a repeat. That document
concludes the jump "cannot police the denominator"; it can, however, corroborate it.

The good population used here is therefore **four independent appearances**:

    B1 = u1      B2 = u2      B3 = u3      B4 = map_full

with **u4 kept as B3's repeat capture** -- a free same-board control that is used in
section 6. `map_full` is promoted from "unit not named in the doc" because it is
measurably distinct (5.98-16.1 grey from every other board).

**Consequence for the project, independent of the anomaly question:** the tolerance
map's population is, by this measurement, at most three distinct boards. The map's
own acceptance ("these four good boards now pass",
`dl/STAGE_I_20261025_tolerance_map.md:193`) is therefore thinner than it reads, and the
"does it generalise to a fifth board" question in that document's section 6 is closer
than it appears.

## 4. Alignment -- measured, and mostly *not* done

The boards are **already co-located**: the part mask's median position is
col 123/124/126/125/125, row 71/70/71/71/71 for u1/u2/u3/u4/map_full. The mask's
*bounding box* differs by up to 128 px, but that is speckle, a failure mode already
recorded in `dl/PART_MASK_PERFRAME_20261026.md:64-68`.

Registering anyway made the match **worse**, for every pair, with two independent
registration models (`dl/analysis/align_check.py`):

| pair | raw | ECC affine | ECC homography |
|---|---|---|---|
| u1-u2 | 11.45 | 16.14 | 11.72 |
| u1-u3 | 10.69 | 14.75 | 30.51 |
| u1-map_full | 7.69 | 9.66 | 9.23 |

The ECC code itself is sound -- it recovers injected transforms to **0.029 px worst
corner error** over four known shifts/rotations. So this is a statement about the data:
there is nothing geometric left to fix, and a registration that increases the residual
is fitting noise.

What *is* corrected is the residual placement, by a **whole-cell integer shift**
(no interpolation): u2 (-2,+1), u3 (-4,-1), u4 (-4,-1), map_full (-1,0), contam (+2,+1)
cells, chosen per capture by the residual the scoring uses (`dl/analysis/align.py`).

**The population spread that matters, and it is not geometry:** a good board sits
**6-9 grey** (cell level) from a *different* good board, against a **1.3-1.6 grey**
same-board floor and a **30 grey** defect threshold. That ratio is the whole difficulty,
and it is why the tolerance map needed 75 relaxed cells (0.52%) and still rescued only
1 of 9 cells on an unseen board.

## 5. Descriptor and models -- what was chosen, and why it costs what it costs

**Descriptor** (4 values per 8x8 cell, `dl/analysis/anom_core.py:descriptor`):

| dim | what | why |
|---|---|---|
| `mu8` | local mean | level |
| `sd8` | local std | texture energy -- a *change of structure* that a mean cancels |
| `mu16` | mean at double scale | a 1-2 px defect moves `mu8` and barely moves `mu16` |
| `mu8-mu16` | local contrast vs neighbourhood | scale-free; cannot be satisfied by a lighting shift |

This is a deliberate subset of the brief's candidate list. Local binary patterns and
orientation histograms were **not** used: 8 comparisons per pixel for LBP and a bin
accumulation per pixel for HoG are 2-4x the arithmetic of the moment statistics, and
the measured bottleneck is not descriptor capacity but the **6-9 grey population
spread** (section 4). A richer descriptor cannot lower that floor. `dl/analysis/cost.py`
prices the four-moment descriptor at **2.34 ops/px** over the part region.

**Models.** Both are used, because they fail differently:

* **PaDiM-style**: per-cell mean from the training boards, **one pooled variance per
  descriptor dimension**. Pooling is forced: with three training boards a per-cell
  variance has 2 degrees of freedom, which is not an estimate; pooling over ~800 cells
  x 3 boards gives ~2400 samples. This is a consequence of the population size, not a
  tuning choice.
* **PatchCore-style**: a 512-entry random coreset of training descriptors, distance to
  the nearest entry.

**The decision structure is the shipped rule's, on purpose.** All three methods use the
same 8x8 grid, the same part mask, the same 8-connected component rule and the same
limit of 8. They differ in exactly one thing -- the per-cell statistic:

    shipped     |live - ref| > 30 grey              (fixed, uses the stored reference)
    PaDiM       Mahalanobis score  > tau            (learned, reference-free)
    PatchCore   kNN distance       > tau            (learned, reference-free)

`tau` is calibrated per method so that **no good board produces an 8-connected group
of 8** -- both anomaly arms therefore have a false-NG of 0/4 at this operating point,
which is *generous to them* because the calibration sees all four boards.

### 5.1 A measurement artefact that had to be thrown away -- recorded, not hidden

The first version scored a frame by the **maximum** per-cell anomaly score. At the
zero-false-NG operating point that maximum is by construction whatever single cell on
the worst good board deviates most, so the threshold saturates. Measured: on B1 the
PaDiM frame score read **191.69 = the threshold** for an injected blob of 6, 8, 12, 16,
24, 32 **and** 48 px -- the injection could not change the number at all. Any
"minimum detectable size" from that statistic would have been an artefact of the frame
statistic. The frame statistic was therefore replaced by the equal-area rule above.

## 6. Leave-one-board-out

Train on three good boards, test on the fourth, threshold from the training boards
only (the largest score they produce).

| held out | PaDiM tau | held-out group | verdict | PatchCore tau | held-out group | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 = u1 | 56.22 | 5 | pass | 7.65 | 6 | pass |
| B2 = u2 | 92.51 | **9** | **false NG** | 9.11 | 5 | pass |
| B3 = u3 | 70.91 | **11** | **false NG** | 4.17 | **17** | **false NG** |
| B4 = map_full | 53.44 | **13** | **false NG** | 5.69 | **8** | **false NG** |
| | | **3 of 4 false NG** | | | **2 of 4 false NG** | |

Against that, the shipped rule on the same four captures, with its shipped constants
and no tuning: group 1, 4, 3, **8** -- one false NG (`map_full`, exactly at the limit).

**The anomaly models do not generalise on four boards.** With a threshold that is
honest (training boards only) they flag 2-3 of 4 unseen good boards. Their 0/4 number
in section 7 only exists because the calibration is allowed to see all four boards
including the one being tested; that is an oracle, and it is reported as one. The
oracle operating point is tau = **55.25** for PaDiM (largest groups 3, 4, 2, 5 on
B1..B4) and tau = **11.20** for PatchCore (groups 2, 2, 1, 7) -- both under the limit
of 8 by construction, and PatchCore's B4 at 7 shows how little headroom the oracle
itself has.

**The same-board control passes, so this is a generalisation failure and not a broken
model**: B3's repeat capture u4 gives group 3 (PaDiM) and 1 (PatchCore) at the
population calibration -- inside the limit, as a repeat of a training board should be.

## 7. The contaminated board

`.tmp/stG/accept1` (the good-reference / contaminated-live pair), 309 judged cells:

| method | largest 8-connected group | limit | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipped rule | **9** | 8 | **caught, margin 1 cell** |
| PaDiM | 40 | 8 | caught, margin 32 |
| PatchCore | **6** | 8 | **missed** |

PatchCore's miss is the sharpest single fact in this document: the one capture of a
genuinely defective board that exists on disk is **below the limit** for the
PatchCore-style detector while it is above the limit for the rule. And PaDiM's large
margin (40) is not evidence of strength -- the same detector flagged two good boards
with groups of 9 and 13 under its honest threshold.

Note the shipped rule's margin here is **one cell**. That is the real operating margin
on this defect, and it is consistent with
`dl/STAGE_H_20261025_registration_accuracy.md:291-296`, which found the good maximum
(8) and the defective minimum (9) adjacent.

## 8. Minimum detectable defect size -- the key claim

Method: inject a defect of known size and contrast into a held-out good frame, with the
**repo's own stamp convention** (`ps_ui/st8_fine_grid.py:107`, integer add to all three
channels, clipped), inside the part's interior mask, at 8 sites per board per condition,
4 boards. Painting is `stamp`'s convention unchanged; the *site finder* is new
(`dl/analysis/anom_run.py:interior_sites`) because `find_stamp_site` guarantees
containment for a **square** only -- its span is `w // cell`, so a 2x48 px stamp is
checked as if it were one cell tall, and scratches would land half off the part.

Detection = a **flip to NG from that board's own un-injected verdict**, so a method that
already fails a good board is not credited with detecting a defect on it.

**Blob (square, contrast +/-60 grey):**

| side | area | shipped rule | PaDiM | PatchCore |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 - 20 px | up to 400 px2 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| 24 px | 576 px2 | **0.83** | 0.03 / 0.09 | 0.12 |
| 32 px | 1024 px2 | **0.88** | 0.50 / 0.62 | 0.50 / 0.69 |
| 40 px | 1600 px2 | **0.96** | 0.84 / 0.75 | 0.75 / 0.97 |

(Bright / dark. Rates are over the boards that passed un-injected: 3 for the rule, 4
for each model, x 8 sites.)

**Thin scratch (48, 96, 144 px long; 2-8 px wide; contrast +/-60 grey):** the shipped
rule detects a **2x96 px** scratch at **0.88-0.92** and a 2x144 px scratch at
0.96-1.00. Neither anomaly model exceeds **0.19** at any width or length tested.

This is the decisive row, and there is a clean reason for it. A w x L scratch covers
about L/8 cells in a line, so **L must exceed 64 px before any rule requiring 8
connected cells can fire at any contrast**. At L = 48 px the shipped rule's 0.00-0.04
is its geometric floor, not a sensitivity limit. Given a scratch long enough to be
reachable, the rule finds a **2-pixel-wide** one; the anomaly models do not find an
8-pixel-wide one.

**Contrast at fixed size (|amp| needed for 50% detection):**

| size | shipped | PaDiM | PatchCore |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24x24 | **45 grey** | 90 | 90 |
| 40x40 | **30 grey** | 60 | 60 |

**Summary of the minimum detectable sizes at the 0-false-NG operating point**, in the
part's own units (0.175 x 0.25 mm/px, `ps_ui/st8_fine_grid.py:44`, so one 8x8 cell is
1.4 x 2.0 mm):

| method | min blob (50%) | min blob (90%) | min scratch width |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipped | **24 px = 4.2 x 6.0 mm** | 40 px = 7.0 x 10.0 mm | **2 px (at 96 px length)** |
| PaDiM | 32 px = 5.6 x 8.0 mm | not reached up to 40 px | never (up to 8 px x 144 px) |
| PatchCore | 32 px = 5.6 x 8.0 mm | 40 px = 7.0 x 10.0 mm | never (up to 8 px x 144 px) |

**Why the models lose, in one number.** The shipped rule compares a board against a
reference of *that board*: its per-cell residual noise is a few grey. An anomaly model
compares a board against *other boards*: the good population's own spread is
**6-9 grey** (section 4). The defect signal has to be found on top of the larger
number. No choice of descriptor or covariance changes that; only a larger population,
or a reference, does. This is the same mechanism that made the tolerance map fail to
generalise -- measured again, from a different direction, and with the same conclusion.

## 9. Cost

Analytic, from the code that runs here (`dl/analysis/cost.py`); registration is charged
to neither side because the shipped loop already runs it.

| stage | ops/frame | ops/px |
|---|---|---|
| shipped fine pass (whole 160x90 lattice) | 5,227,200 | 5.67 |
| descriptor (part region, 2145 cells) | 321,750 | 2.34 |
| PaDiM score | 102,960 | 0.75 |
| **PaDiM total** | **424,710 = 0.08x** | |
| PatchCore score, 512-entry coreset | 13,178,880 | 96.00 |
| **PatchCore total** | **13,500,630 = 2.58x** | |

Every one of the 512 coreset entries is compared against every judged cell, so PatchCore
costs **2.6x the entire shipped fine pass** -- that branch is not portable at any
accuracy. PaDiM is **12x cheaper** than the fine pass, and its cost is therefore *not*
the reason it was rejected; its accuracy is.

Anchors to judge those against, all measured on the board and quoted with their source:
comparison 91 ms (2026-10-24 build) and **167 ms** (2026-10-26 build,
`dl/XCHECK_20261026.md:137`); loop 104.9 ms with the per-loop mask publisher off and
138.0 ms with it on (`dl/PART_MASK_PERFRAME_20261026.md:107`).

CPython wall clock is in `dl/analysis/cost.json` and is a **relative** indicator only --
it is not a Cortex-A9 and must not be read as an on-board time. No on-board timing was
taken, because the board was not touched.

## 10. Conclusion, stated no stronger than the evidence

**"Loses, do not port."**

1. **The anomaly models do not generalise** on this data: 3 of 4 (PaDiM) and 2 of 4
   (PatchCore) unseen good boards are flagged under a threshold that never saw them. The
   shipped rule flags 1 of 4 by construction (it is a fixed threshold, not a learned one).
2. **They do not detect smaller defects.** The rule's 24 px blob and 2x96 px scratch
   beat both models' 32 px and "never". The claim the feature was proposed to make is
   refuted, and by a margin that is not marginal (24 vs 32 px at 50%, and a complete
   failure on scratches).
3. **PatchCore misses the only contaminated board on disk.**
4. **The cost is not a saving for the branch that would matter**: PatchCore is 2.58x
   the fine pass. PaDiM is 0.08x, so if only cost mattered it would be attractive -- but
   at 3/4 false-NG it cannot be deployed.
5. What survives as a *useful* result is the diagnosis: the good population's spread
   (6-9 grey at cell level, measured three independent ways in section 4) is of the same
   order as the defect signal, so any detector that does not use a per-board reference
   is fighting a 3-5x worse noise floor than the rule's. That is why the tolerance map
   did not generalise, and it predicts that an anomaly model will not either -- which is
   what was then measured.

If the idea is revisited, the only version worth trying is one that has **more good
boards** (the pooled variance and the 6-9 grey spread are both functions of having
three), or one that **uses the reference** -- but a detector that uses the reference is
the shipped rule with a learned tolerance, which is
`dl/STAGE_I_20261025_tolerance_map.md` and already exists.

## 11. What remains unverified

1. **Nothing was measured on the board.** All times and all op counts are analytic or
   off-board. The claim "PaDiM adds about 0.08x the fine pass" is an op-count ratio, not
   a measurement of the loop.
2. **The contaminated board was evaluated against the fixed 30-grey rule**, not against
   the four-unit tolerance map that production runs. The map's frame is not on disk
   (section 1.2). Its effect on this comparison is unmeasured; it would relax 75 of
   14,400 cells and could only raise the shipped rule's margin.
3. **The shipped rule's 1/4 false-NG is a single-frame replay, not the firmware's
   averaged comparison.** `ps_ui/stI_replay.py:19-23` states this is an upper bound on
   cells over threshold, so the firmware's own false-NG could be lower --
   `dl/STAGE_I_20261025_tolerance_map.md:113` measured 0 NG over 181 comparisons on the
   four units with the map in force. The 1/4 in this report is not directly comparable
   to that 0.
4. **`MB_TOL_N = 4` is not independently verified.** Section 3 shows u3 and u4 are one
   board by appearance; it cannot say whether the operator placed one board twice or
   whether two visually identical units exist. The population count is therefore an
   inference from 1.58 grey, strong but not the operator's bookkeeping.
5. **`map_full` is unlabelled** in every source document. It is used as B4 because it is
   measurably distinct, not because anyone recorded which unit it is.
6. **Only one contaminated board exists on disk.** Its group of 9 is one frame pair.
   Nothing here says how the rule or the models would do over a population of defective
   parts, and the 1-cell margin is exactly where a second sample could reverse a verdict.
7. **The descriptor was not searched.** Four moments were chosen and priced; LBP, HoG
   and multi-scale variants were rejected on cost grounds without being run. A richer
   descriptor would raise the models' scores but cannot lower the 6-9 grey population
   floor, and that floor is what the conclusion rests on.
8. **The 24-px figure is the rule's arithmetic floor as well as its measured one** (a
   defect must cover at least 8 connected cells, so a square needs about 22.6 px). The
   measurement agreeing with the arithmetic is a consistency check, not an independent
   discovery.
